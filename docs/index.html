Designed for research workflows

Control the transcription, then check the result.

RedScribe creates a draft that remains editable. The researcher verifies the transcript and report against the original recording.

01

Choose a model

Faster-Whisper large-v3 is the default. Turbo, medium, small and base let users choose for available hardware.

02

GPU or CPU

Windows can use NVIDIA CUDA with automatic CPU fallback. Apple Silicon uses local CPU processing and has been tested on a Mac mini M4 with 24 GB unified memory.

03

Set the language

Use automatic detection or select Bahasa Melayu or English when detection is inaccurate. Speech is transcribed, not translated.

04

Identify speakers

Optional pyannote.audio diarisation adds speaker labels. An expected number of speakers can also be supplied.

05

Follow progress

See a live transcript, estimated timestamps, detected language, progress and remaining time. Stop before export begins.

06

Check token availability

A local check reports whether a Hugging Face token is available without displaying it. Model access is not tested by this check.

What users receive

Two files from each completed run.

RedScribe saves matching, numbered .xlsx and .docx files locally. The deposit itself contains source code; users generate their own reports from their recordings.

EXCEL WORKBOOK · .XLSX

Structured review and early report

  • Early_Report: study and processing metadata, model and CPU/GPU mode, language, diarisation status and processing time.
  • Segment and utterance counts, simple word/token counts, estimated speech and silence durations, and speaker utterance counts.
  • Speech versus silence chart, five-minute utterance density chart, and top 20 token frequencies.
  • Transcription: one utterance per row with speaker, start/end times, automated text, and blank verification/correction and notes columns.
WORD REPORT · .DOCX

Editable report and readable transcript

  • Opening report with study metadata and transcription summary.
  • Speaker counts, speech versus silence table and chart, five-minute timeline table and chart, and top 20 token table.
  • A separate transcript section after a page break.
  • Adjacent utterances by the same speaker grouped into paragraphs with speaker labels and estimated start/end times.

Counts are simple text frequencies, and timing and speaker assignments are estimates. Always compare outputs with the recording before research use.

Better audio, better transcripts

Recording tips for better transcription.

Automated transcription and speaker identification depend heavily on the quality of the original recording. A few simple precautions can substantially improve the result.

01

Choose a suitable environment

Whenever possible, record in a quiet, enclosed room rather than a busy public area. Reduce noise from conversations, traffic, fans, air conditioning and music. Curtains, carpets and soft furnishings can also help reduce echo.

02

Keep voices clear and close

Place the recording device close enough to capture every participant clearly. A smartphone can work well when positioned appropriately in a quiet environment. Speak at a normal conversational volume; excessive volume may distort the audio.

03

Position group microphones properly

For group discussions, use a suitable omnidirectional or conference microphone near the centre of the group. Keep every participant within its effective pickup range. Larger groups or wider seating arrangements may require more than one microphone.

04

Test before starting

Record and review a short sample before the session. Confirm that every participant can be heard, then check the battery, available storage and recording settings. Use headphones when possible to detect background noise, echo or microphone problems.

05

Avoid interruptions and handling noise

Place the device on a stable surface and avoid moving or touching it during the session. Silence notifications or enable airplane mode when appropriate, and keep papers, drinks and other objects away from the microphone.

06

Always make a backup recording

Research interviews may contain unique data that cannot be recreated. When possible, use a dedicated recorder as the primary device and a smartphone as the backup. Confirm that both timers are running, obtain appropriate consent and store the recordings securely.

Get started

Run RedScribe on your computer.

RedScribe supports Windows 10 or 11 and Apple Silicon macOS. Python 3.10 or 3.11 and FFmpeg are required. The README provides separate Windows and macOS installation instructions, model selection guidance and optional Hugging Face diarisation setup. Audio processing is local; initial model downloads and authentication may require internet access.
